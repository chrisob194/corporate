# Design — #42

## Problem

A role asked to specify or design PDF output has no team-side answer for any of it: how to choose a generation approach, how non-Latin text survives the round trip, how a page is set up, where lines break, what actually makes a byte stream a PDF, or how a test asserts on generated output. `technical-architect` can pay for a live sweep; `builder`, `reviewer` and `qa-engineer` hold no `WebSearch` and would answer from memory — which is the one thing every playbook exists to forbid (`plugins/corporate/reference/stack-readiness.md:9-17`). Issue #42 closes that gap by authoring the nineteenth stack playbook skill: `pdf-playbook`.

The deliverable is one plugin component — a `SKILL.md` plus one `references/` sibling — and the repo's own record of what ships. In scope: the five topics the spec names (selection method including maintenance-state checking, non-Latin text with one worked language, page setup, line breaking **across lines and across pages**, byte-stream validity) plus the automated-assertion topic, all stated so a reader with no network can apply them. The "across pages" half of the spec's requirement 5 is a topic in its own right: where a line breaks is one question, where a *page* breaks is another, and the format answers neither.

Out of scope, per the spec's non-goals: naming a library, tool or product as the answer; any format other than PDF; what any document should contain; storing or delivering the file; and performing the candidate research on a future requester's behalf.

## Approach

One new directory, `plugins/corporate/skills/pdf-playbook/`, holding `SKILL.md` and `references/docs-map.md`. It follows the playbook contract at `docs/authoring.md:102-185` exactly: `name: pdf-playbook` matching the directory (enforced at `scripts/validate.ts:201-214`), a "Use when …" description naming the format's *identifiers* rather than its marketing name, and the five body sections in the fixed order `## Stack`, `## Toolchain`, `## Obligations by activity`, `## Traps`, `## Resources` (`docs/authoring.md:132-146`). Seven rulings shape it.

**1. The stack is the format, not a package.** Every other playbook's `## Stack` names packages to import (`docs/authoring.md:136`). Here that is forbidden by the spec's requirement 2, and the conflict is real rather than cosmetic. It resolves without bending the contract: the stack genuinely *is* PDF — a byte format fixed by ISO 32000-2 (F1–F4) that no library changes — and the candidate that writes it is a per-project decision, ruled in the design. That is the shape `crypto-playbook` already ships: "Cryptography is chosen, never built… which binding a project uses is a per-project decision, ruled in the design. This playbook is the engine, not a binding" (`plugins/corporate/skills/crypto-playbook/SKILL.md:10-12`). So `## Stack` states the format facts flat and then the four candidate *families*, and it names no package at all. The stack identifier for a `## Stack readiness` row is `pdf`.

**2. Requirement 9 — surviving candidate churn — is answered by a command, not by a list.** A playbook that enumerated today's candidates would need rewriting the week one of them stalled. Instead the maintenance check is two runnable commands over data that is always current (F14, F15): `bun info <pkg>` for licence, dependency count, dist-tags, maintainers and publish date, `bun info <pkg> time.modified` for the raw timestamp, and `gh repo view <owner>/<repo> --json pushedAt,isArchived,licenseInfo,latestRelease` for the repository side. Both were verified on this machine. A method expressed as commands cannot rot into an endorsement, which is exactly what requirements 2 and 9 ask for together.

**3. Japanese is the worked non-Latin case, and its failure mode is demonstrated rather than described.** One concrete language, per the spec's assumption. Japanese earns it because it exercises three of the spec's topics at once — composite fonts (F5), text extractability (F8) and line breaking with no inter-word spaces (F10) — where a Latin-adjacent script would exercise one. The worked case is F7: an empirically produced PDF that is structurally valid, raises no error anywhere, and extracts as `æ—¥æœ¬èª`.

**4. The pinned doc source is two URLs, and the sibling carries the rest.** `docs/authoring.md:172-181` caps `SKILL.md` at two URLs and permits a `references/` sibling where the upstream tree is too large for its index to resolve a question. ISO 32000-2 is roughly a thousand pages behind a registration form — the limiting case that clause was written for. So `SKILL.md`'s single `any` obligation row names `pdfa.org/sponsored-standards` (ISO 32000-2 at no cost, F1) and `unicode.org/reports/tr14` (line breaking, which PDF does not define, F9), plus the sibling by name; `references/docs-map.md` maps each decision this team makes to its exact ISO clause and lists the second-tier normative sources — JLReq, CSS Paged Media, the tool manuals — alongside, which is the shape `plugins/corporate/skills/crypto-playbook/references/docs-map.md:1-8` already ships.

**5. Neither a line break nor a page break is the format's job.** Settled here rather than left to the build: PDF places glyphs at coordinates and has no concept of a line, so "how does text wrap" (spec requirement 5, first half) is a question about Unicode and about which candidate family you picked, not about PDF. That is why UAX #14 is the second pinned URL and not a footnote. **The same argument runs one level up and the first pass of this design failed to run it.** PDF has no concept of a page *flow* either: a page object holds a content stream and nothing carries content from one to the next, so "how does text wrap across pages" (requirement 5, second half) is answered by the generator tracking a cursor and emitting a new page, exactly as a margin is answered by the generator computing a coordinate (F5). Content placed past the page box is discarded by the format's own rule and is lost in silence (F18). This ruling was amended on the design redo of 2026-09-14, after review found requirement 5's "across pages" clause reached nothing in the deliverable (`docs/corporate/42/review.md:47-60`); F18 is the fact set's answer, and the rest of the design is unchanged.

**6. One toolchain, and the inspection tools degrade gracefully.** Package commands are `bun` only; `npm`, `npx`, `yarn` and `pnpm` are banned outright (`docs/authoring.md:182-185`). The inspection CLIs (`qpdf`, `pdftotext`, `pdffonts`, `pdfinfo`, `verapdf`) are a second, separate matter: they may be absent from a CI image, so the playbook states the in-process fallback — assert on the bytes directly (F2) — and states that a missing tool fails a suite rather than skipping it.

**7. No reverse cross-link into a sibling playbook.** #37 added one because Angular and Tailwind genuinely meet (`docs/corporate/37/design.md`, ruling 4). Nothing here does: generating a document does not enter the Bun, TypeScript or GitHub playbooks' areas, and a link claiming otherwise would be a summary that rots. `pdf-playbook` lists them under its own `## Resources`; they list nothing back.

### Pinned PDF facts (F1–F18)

Every fact the playbook states is fixed here with the source it came from, fetched or executed on 2026-09-14. This list is the authority T1 transcribes; nothing about PDF is left for the build to decide. **A fact the file needs that is absent here is a design defect to route back, never a licence to write it from memory.**

- **F1 — Authority.** ISO 32000-2:2020 is the current PDF specification (PDF 2.0); ISO 32000-1:2008 is PDF 1.7. The PDF Association publishes it at no cost, sponsored by Adobe, Apryse and Foxit, at `pdfa.org/sponsored-standards` — the bundle carries ISO 32000-2:2020, its Amd 1, and ISO/TS 32001/32002. Formalized errata are published per clause at `pdf-issues.pdfa.org/32000-2-2020/`. Sources: `pdfa.org/announcing-no-cost-access-to-iso-32000-2-pdf-2-0/`, `pdf-issues.pdfa.org/32000-2-2020/clause09.html`.
- **F2 — The minimal valid byte stream.** Four parts in order: a header line `%PDF-n.m` as the very first bytes; a second-line comment of at least four bytes with codes ≥ 128, which marks the file binary; a body of numbered indirect objects (`N 0 obj … endobj`); a cross-reference section listing each object's byte offset; and a trailer dictionary followed by `startxref`, the byte offset of the cross-reference section, and `%%EOF`. Verified empirically: a hand-assembled 669-byte file — Catalog → Pages → Page → content stream → font, `%PDF-1.7` header, `%\xe2\xe3\xcf\xd3` binary comment, classic `xref` table, trailer `<< /Size 6 /Root 1 0 R /ID [<…> <…>] >>`, `startxref 410`, `%%EOF` — is accepted by `pdfinfo` (reports 1 page, A4, PDF version 1.7) and `pdftotext`. Built and run on this machine, 2026-09-14.
- **F3 — Trailer dictionary keys.** `/Size` (integer) and `/Root` (the document catalogue) are required. `/ID` (array) is required since PDF 2.0, and whenever `/Encrypt` is present. `/Info` is required conditionally (since PDF 1.4, when `/PieceInfo` is in the catalogue). `/Encrypt`, `/Prev` and `/XRefStm` are optional; `/Prev` is what chains an incremental update to the previous cross-reference section. Source: Arlington PDF Model `tsv/latest/FileTrailer.tsv` (`github.com/pdf-association/arlington-pdf-model`).
- **F4 — Page object keys.** Required: `/Type` (value `Page` or `Template`), `/Parent`, `/Resources`, `/MediaBox`. `/Resources` and `/MediaBox` are **inheritable from the parent page-tree node** — a page can omit them and still be valid. `/CropBox` is optional and defaults to `@MediaBox`. `/Rotate` is optional, defaults to `0`, and is constrained by `fn:Eval((@Rotate mod 90)==0)` — only multiples of 90. `/Contents` is optional (a stream or an array of streams). Source: Arlington PDF Model `tsv/latest/PageObject.tsv`. The `/CropBox` default was independently confirmed on this machine: `pdfinfo -box` on a hand-built file declaring only `/MediaBox [0 0 595.276 841.89]` prints `CropBox`, `BleedBox`, `TrimBox` and `ArtBox` all equal to it.
- **F5 — Units and page geometry.** The default user space unit is 1/72 inch, with the origin at the bottom-left corner of the page; `/MediaBox` is `[llx lly urx ury]` in those units. A4 is 595.276 × 841.89 units (confirmed: `pdfinfo` on the F2 file printed `Page size: 595.276 x 841.89 pts (A4)`); US Letter is 612 × 792, i.e. 8.5 × 11 inches at 72 units to the inch. **Nothing in the format expresses a margin** — a margin exists only in whatever computes coordinates before writing them.
- **F6 — Simple fonts are single-byte.** A simple font maps one byte to one glyph, so it addresses at most 256 characters, selected by an encoding name such as `/WinAnsiEncoding`. Text outside that repertoire cannot be addressed at all. Composite (Type 0) fonts exist for everything else: `/Subtype /Type0` with required `/BaseFont`, `/Encoding` and `/DescendantFonts`, and optional `/ToUnicode`. `/Encoding` is a CMap — either a stream or one of the predefined names, which include `Identity-H` and `Identity-V` alongside the CJK families (`UniJIS-UCS2-H/V`, `UniJIS-UTF16-H/V`, `90ms-RKSJ-H/V`, `UniGB-UCS2-H/V`, `UniCNS-UCS2-H/V`, `KSCms-UHC-H/V`, and others). Source: Arlington PDF Model `tsv/latest/FontType0.tsv`.
- **F7 — The worked failure, verified.** A PDF placing the UTF-8 bytes of `日本語` into a content-stream string with `/BaseFont /Helvetica /Encoding /WinAnsiEncoding` is structurally valid, produces no error and no warning from any tool, and `pdftotext -enc UTF-8` extracts `æ—¥æœ¬èª` — one Latin-1 glyph per UTF-8 byte. `pdffonts` on it prints `Helvetica | Type 1 | WinAnsi | emb no | sub no | uni no`. Built and run on this machine with poppler 24.02.0, 2026-09-14. The silence is the point: nothing in the pipeline fails.
- **F8 — `/ToUnicode` is what makes text extractable.** It is an **optional** stream on a font dictionary mapping character codes to Unicode values; absent it, extraction and search have no way back from glyph codes to characters, which is what the `uni` column of `pdffonts` reports. The ISO 32000-2 errata add Table 125a defining the entries of a ToUnicode CMap stream dictionary (`Type`, `CMapName`, `CIDSystemInfo`, `WMode`, `UseCMap`, all optional). Sources: Arlington `FontType0.tsv`; `pdf-issues.pdfa.org/32000-2-2020/clause09.html`.
- **F9 — The standard 14 fonts are Latin and are scoped to older processors.** The published errata for clause 9 restates the NOTE as: "PDF processors supporting PDF 1.0 to PDF 1.7 files ~~shall~~ *are required to* have these fonts, or their font metrics and suitable substitution fonts available" — the guarantee is written against 1.0–1.7 processors, and the set (the Helvetica, Times and Courier families, Symbol, ZapfDingbats) carries no non-Latin glyphs in any case. Source: `pdf-issues.pdfa.org/32000-2-2020/clause09.html`.
- **F10 — Line breaking is Unicode's, not PDF's.** UAX #14 (revision 55, Unicode 17.0.0, 2025-09-05) "produces a set of 'break opportunities', or positions that would be suitable for wrapping lines when preparing text for display", distinguishing mandatory breaks from permissible ones across roughly 40 line-break classes. For ideographic scripts, "lines can break anywhere, unless prohibited". It is explicitly "a tailorable set of rules" that implementations may modify provided they document the deviation. Source: `unicode.org/reports/tr14/`.
- **F11 — Japanese line breaking specifics.** W3C *Requirements for Japanese Text Layout* (WG Note, 2020-08-11, based on JIS X 4051): Japanese uses no inter-word spacing — "In principle, when composing a line with ideographic, hiragana and katakana characters no extra spacing appears between their character frame" — so a break may fall between almost any two characters. Kinsoku shori constrains this with two prohibition classes, characters that may not start a line (行頭禁則) and characters that may not end a line (行末禁則), typically closing brackets and punctuation. Line adjustment then reduces or adds inter-character spacing to make the line fit. Source: `w3.org/TR/jlreq/`.
- **F12 — "It opens" is not "it is valid", verified.** Taking the valid 669-byte file from F2 and replacing only its `startxref` offset with a bogus `999999` leaves `pdfinfo` and `pdftotext` both exiting 0 and printing correct output — poppler silently reconstructs the cross-reference table by scanning for objects. Run on this machine, 2026-09-14. A viewer or an extractor accepting a file proves nothing about its structure.
- **F13 — The structural check, and its own limit.** `qpdf --check` will "Check the file's structure as well as encryption, linearization, and encoding of stream data, and write information about the file to standard output." Exit codes: `0` = "no errors or warnings were found", `2` = "errors were found; the file was not processed", `3` = "warnings were found without errors"; `--warning-exit-0` collapses 3 to 0. qpdf states its own limit: "A file for which `--check` reports no errors may still have errors in stream data content or may contain constructs that don't conform to the PDF specification." Source: `qpdf.readthedocs.io/en/stable/cli.html`.
- **F14 — The inspection toolchain, verified on poppler 24.02.0.** `pdfinfo f.pdf` prints `Pages`, `Page size` in points with the recognised name, `Page rot`, `Encrypted`, `File size`, `PDF version`; `-box` adds the page bounding boxes, `-isodates` normalises dates, `-meta` dumps XMP. `pdftotext -enc UTF-8 f.pdf -` writes extracted text to stdout; `-layout` preserves the physical layout, `-raw` keeps content-stream order, `-nopgbrk` suppresses the page-break character, `-f`/`-l` bound the page range, `-listenc` lists encodings. `pdffonts f.pdf` prints one row per font with columns `name type encoding emb sub uni object ID` — `emb` = font program embedded, `sub` = subset, `uni` = has a ToUnicode map. Local `-h` output, 2026-09-14.
- **F15 — Conformance validation is a separate tool.** veraPDF validates against PDF/A and PDF/UA: "The veraPDF software comes with predefined sets of rules covering PDF/A and PDF/UA standards." Syntax `verapdf [options] [file or directory]`; `-f`/`--flavour` takes a two-character code (`-f 1b` for PDF/A-1b), `-f 0` auto-detects from the file's metadata, `--defaultflavour`/`-df` sets the fallback, `-p`/`--profile` loads a custom profile, `--recurse` walks a directory. Source: `docs.verapdf.org/cli/validation/`.
- **F16 — Generated PDFs are not byte-reproducible by default.** PDF 2.0 "declares document info entries as deprecated except for the *CreationDate* and *ModDate* entries" (the two that are timestamps), and `/ID` is required from PDF 2.0 onward (F3). So a generated file carries at least a creation timestamp and a file identifier, and two runs of the same generator over the same input differ byte-for-byte unless both are pinned. Sources: `pdflib.com/pdf-knowledge-base/pdf-20/deprecated-features/`; Arlington `FileTrailer.tsv`.
- **F17 — The maintenance-state check, verified on bun 1.3.14.** Run from a directory containing a `package.json`: `bun info <pkg>` prints `name@version | <licence> | deps: n | versions: n`, the description, the homepage, the `dist` block, the full `dist-tags` map, the `maintainers` list and `Published: <ISO-8601>`. `bun info <pkg> <property-path>` prints a single field — `bun info zod time.modified` printed `2026-09-13T23:25:14.950Z` and `bun info zod version` printed `4.6.5`. Outside a project directory it fails with `error: Bun could not find a package.json file to install from`. The registry document behind it holds `dist-tags`, `versions`, `time` ("an object mapping versions to the time published, along with `created` and `modified` timestamps"), `maintainers`, `license` and, where set, `deprecated` (source: `github.com/npm/registry/blob/main/docs/responses/package-metadata.md`). Repository-side: `gh repo view <owner>/<repo> --json …` accepts `pushedAt`, `updatedAt`, `isArchived`, `licenseInfo`, `latestRelease`, `stargazerCount`, `issues` among its fields — verified from `gh repo view --json` with no argument, which prints the valid set. Run on this machine, 2026-09-14.
- **F18 — Content outside the page box is discarded, and the format expresses no page flow. Added on the design redo of 2026-09-14.**

  *Normative half.* ISO 32000-1:2008 clause 14.11.2.1, "Page Boundaries — General", quoted verbatim from the publicly hosted specification PDF (`opensource.adobe.com/dc-acrobat-sdk-docs/standards/pdfstandards/pdf/PDF32000_2008.pdf`, downloaded and extracted with `pdftotext` on this machine, 2026-09-14): of the media box — *"The media box defines the boundaries of the physical medium on which the page is to be printed… Content falling outside this boundary may safely be discarded without affecting the meaning of the PDF file."* Of the crop box — *"The crop box defines the region to which the contents of the page shall be clipped (cropped) when displayed or printed. Unlike the other boxes, the crop box has no defined meaning in terms of physical page geometry or intended use; it merely imposes clipping on the page contents… The default value is the page's media box."* And: *"The crop, bleed, trim, and art boxes shall not ordinarily extend beyond the boundaries of the media box. If they do, they are effectively reduced to their intersection with the media box."* The clause number is the same in ISO 32000-2 and the published errata index carries no formalized item against 14.11.2 (`pdf-issues.pdfa.org/32000-2-2020/clause14.html`, fetched 2026-09-14 — its 14.11 entries are 14.11.5 and 14.11.7 only). So: the visible region of a page is its crop box, which by default is its media box (F4), and a mark placed outside it is clipped away by the format's own rule. Nothing about this is a defect, an error, or a condition any producer is obliged to report.

  *Empirical half — this machine, 2026-09-14, poppler 24.02.0 and Ghostscript.* Two A4 files were hand-assembled by the same method as F2, differing **only** in their content stream: one draws a single 24 pt string at `72 700 Td`; the other draws that same string plus three more, at `72 -100 Td` (below the page), `72 2000 Td` (above it) and `900 400 Td` (right of it).
  1. Rendered with `gs -sDEVICE=png16m -r72`, the two files produce **byte-identical PNGs** (same md5). The three out-of-box strings put no mark on the page.
  2. `pdfinfo` on the overflowing file exits 0 and reports `Pages: 1`, `Page size: 595.276 x 841.89 pts (A4)`. No warning, no diagnostic.
  3. `pdftotext -enc UTF-8` on it exits 0, writes **zero bytes to stderr**, and extracts only `VISIBLE ROW ONE`. The same is true with `-raw` and with `-layout`. The three out-of-box strings do not appear in the extraction at all.
  4. Changing **only** the `/MediaBox` to `[-500 -500 2500 2500]`, content stream untouched, makes all four strings extract. So the box — not the stream — is what removed them.
  5. The boundary case is worse than the clear-cut one. A string whose baseline straddles the edge (`72 -5 Td`, 24 pt, so its ascenders sit inside the box) renders **differently** from the in-box-only file (different md5 — the glyph tops are drawn and clipped at the page edge) and is **still not extracted at all**. Poppler's extraction keys on the text origin, not on the glyph bounding box: half-visible and wholly unextractable at once.
  6. `qpdf` is not installed on this machine, so its behaviour here was not executed. It is reasoned from F13's own statement of what `--check` examines — file structure, encryption, linearization and stream *encoding* — none of which is where in user space a mark was placed. The playbook must state this half as the inference it is.

  *This team's derivation from F5 and the above.* PDF expresses no page flow, no column and no "next page" any more than it expresses a margin. A page object owns a content stream and nothing carries content from one page object to the next. Pagination — tracking a vertical cursor down the content area, deciding where a block breaks, emitting a new page object, resetting the cursor, repeating running heads — belongs entirely to whatever computes coordinates, which in the direct-construction family is the calling code and in the browser-engine family is the engine's CSS Paged Media implementation. Consequently overflow is silent in *both* directions and defeats every assertion the `testing` obligation prescribes: structure passes, the page count reads as expected, and the extracted text simply lacks the rows that fell off. The detection is positive rather than negative — assert the expected number of pages, and assert that the **last** expected string extracts. A test that checks only the header passes on a document whose final rows nobody can see.

Two further framings are this team's own, derived from the facts above rather than fetched, and the playbook states them as such. **The candidate families**: (a) direct construction — code emits PDF objects, so `/MediaBox`, fonts, glyph coordinates, every line break *and every page break* are yours (F5, F6, F10, F18); (b) an HTML/CSS engine printing to PDF — layout, shaping, line breaking *and pagination* come from the engine and page setup comes from CSS Paged Media's `@page` and its `size` property (`<length>{1,2} | auto | [<page-size> || [portrait | landscape]]`, initial `auto`, where `auto` means "The page box will be set to a size and orientation chosen by the UA", keywords A5/A4/A3/B5/B4/JIS-B5/JIS-B4/letter/legal/ledger — `drafts.csswg.org/css-page-3/`), at the cost of a browser-class process; (c) a typesetting engine driven as a subprocess — best line breaking and best page breaking, at the cost of a non-JS toolchain in the image; (d) a hosted API — no runtime cost, a network dependency and document content leaving the process. **The selection criteria**: script and font coverage (can it embed an arbitrary TTF/OTF and build a Type 0 font — F6), extractability (does it emit `/ToUnicode` — F8), who breaks lines and who breaks pages (F10, F11, F18), runtime fit (pure JS vs native addon vs browser binary vs subprocess, and whether the deployment target tolerates each), weight and cold start, determinism (F16), licence, maintenance state (F17), and removal cost — how much of your own code speaks the candidate's API.

## Rejected

- **A `references/candidates.md` matrix scoring current libraries.** Loses on requirement 9 and requirement 2 at once: it is stale the week a candidate's maintenance state changes, and a table with a winning row *is* an endorsement however it is captioned. The method (F17) survives churn; a snapshot cannot.
- **Naming one default candidate "unless you have a reason not to".** Directly contradicts requirement 2 and the spec's first non-goal. It is also the failure mode the spec was written against — a choice made once and reused blind.
- **Splitting into `pdf-playbook` and `pdf-testing-playbook`.** `docs/authoring.md:111-116` splits by the areas upstream already uses; ISO 32000-2 is one tree, and assertion is one `## Obligations by activity` row plus three `## Toolchain` rows, not a second component.
- **Folding the guidance into `typescript-playbook` or `bun-runtime-playbook`.** `docs/authoring.md:108-110` — one playbook per stack, because a narrow description routes better. PDF is not a TypeScript question and would never be reached from that trigger.
- **No `references/` sibling, SKILL.md alone.** Rejected here though it was right for #37: ISO 32000-2 is ~1000 pages behind a registration form, so the index alone does not resolve "which clause says a page may inherit its MediaBox". That is the exact case `docs/authoring.md:176-179` permits a sibling for, and it is also what keeps `SKILL.md` inside its two-URL cap.
- **A worked example in a script rather than a language.** An abstract list of scripts was considered and loses to the spec's own assumption; one language demonstrated end to end (F7, F11) teaches the failure mode, a list of script names teaches nothing.
- **Add a dependency (priced explicitly).** Nothing to add. A PDF library as a design-time fixture was the only plausible case and lost to hand-assembled bytes, which proved the same facts, named no candidate and left nothing installed.
- **On the redo — a new `## Obligations by activity` row for pagination, or a rewrite of the `testing` row.** Considered and rejected as scope the redo does not need. The obligation table's `implementing` row already carries "page size, orientation and margins fixed in writing before content is placed"; the detection instruction F18 yields ("assert the page count, and assert the *last* expected string extracts") is one clause and rides the trap row, where the failure it detects is described. Reopening a row the reviewer verified against F1–F17 (`docs/corporate/42/review.md:66-68`) would put a verified transcription back in play for no gain.
- **On the redo — a second worked example file demonstrating page overflow.** `docs/authoring.md` permits one `references/` sibling for a doc map, not a fixture library. F18 carries the reproduction in the design, where the build reads it; the playbook states the fact, as it does for F7 and F12.

## Risks

- **Requirement 2 is a judgement, and only half of it is mechanical.** The acceptance lines catch the obvious violations — a `bun add` row, a vendor domain — but "reads as a method, not an endorsement" is a human reading, which is why the spec's own loop hint puts a person at that gate once. The reviewer inherits it, and it held on the first pass (`docs/corporate/42/review.md:62-64`).
- **The maintenance-check commands are the one part that can rot.** `bun info <pkg> <property>` is verified on bun 1.3.14; a future bun could move it. Mitigated by the registry document behind it being pinned in F17, so the fallback is stated rather than discovered.
- **The inspection CLIs are not guaranteed present.** Half of them are absent from this very machine. The playbook states the in-process fallback for exactly that reason; a build that forgets it ships guidance that fails in a lean CI image.
- **F7, F12 and F18's empirical half are observed, not quoted from the standard.** They are reproducible (the commands are in the design) but they describe how poppler 24.02.0 and Ghostscript behave, not what ISO 32000-2 requires. The playbook must state them as the observable behaviour they are. F18 is the mixed case and the most exposed: its *clipping* half is normative and quoted (ISO 32000-1 §14.11.2.1), its *extraction* half is poppler's choice to key on the text origin, and its `qpdf --check` half is neither — it is an inference from F13's statement of scope, since qpdf is not installed here. A build that flattens the three into one confident sentence overstates the last of them.
- **F18's extraction finding cuts the other way for a reader who over-reads it.** Because poppler drops out-of-box text from extraction, a `pdftotext`-based assertion *can* catch a lost row — but only if it asserts on the content of that row. The trap must therefore state the detection positively (page count plus last expected string), or a reader concludes extraction is a general safety net, which it is not: the straddling case renders a half-visible row and extracts nothing for it either way.
